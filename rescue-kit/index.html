Contract Rescue Kit

Fill in the [brackets]. Edit anything. Print this page to save a PDF. These are general templates, not legal advice; check your contract before changing terms.

1. One-page bid check

  1. Labor hours per month you expect: ______ . Add 10%: ______ . Does the job still make money?
  2. Break-even price (covers direct cost and overhead): $______
  3. Price at your target margin: $______ . Your quote: $______
  4. If your quote is under the target price, what will you cut (scope, frequency) or ask for (price)?
  5. Walk-away price written down before you negotiate: $______

2. Price increase letter

Subject: Update to our service pricing effective [date]

Hi [name],

Thank you for [working with us / trusting us with your property]. Starting [date], our price for [service] at [location] will change from [old price] to [new price] per [month/visit]. This reflects [higher labor and supply costs / the actual time the work requires]. [Optional: Here is what we can do to keep the price lower: reduce [frequency/scope] to [option].]

If you would like to talk it through, I am at [phone] on [days]. We would like to keep working with you.

[Your name, business]

3. Scope or hours adjustment letter

Subject: Adjusting the scope for [location]

Hi [name],

Over the last [period] we have averaged [actual hours] hours per [visit/month] against the [planned hours] we planned for. The difference comes from [reason: added areas, higher traffic, condition of floors, etc.]. We can either (a) adjust the price to [new price], or (b) return to the original scope by [remove tasks]. Which would you prefer?

[Your name, business]

4. Counter-offer script (for a low offer)

  • "Thanks for the offer. At [their price] I would lose money on this job, so I cannot take it as written."
  • "My price for the full scope is [target price]. If the budget is fixed, I can do [reduced scope] for [price]."
  • "I can start [date] if we agree on [price/scope] today."

Rule: never name a price you cannot support with your own numbers.
